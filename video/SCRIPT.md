# Red Pen: demo video script

Target length: about 2:00 (the rules allow 1 to 3 minutes). Record the voiceover
yourselves. Judges want to hear the students explain their own app.

The rules say the video must include: both participants' names, the app's name,
its purpose in one clear sentence, and a demonstration of what it does.

| Time | Picture | Who | Voiceover |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0:00 – 0:05 | 3D ledger points appear out of the dark | David | "This is five years of spending from one small Iowa town." |
| 0:05 – 0:12 | Camera glides through the ledger | Srihari | "Iowa has 947 cities, and three out of four have fewer than 2,500 people. Most can't afford a financial analyst." |
| 0:12 – 0:16 | Points dim | David | "So no one ever reads every line." |
| 0:16 – 0:22 | Red beam sweeps through, findings get circled | Srihari | "Red Pen does." |
| 0:22 – 0:27 | Overhead shot, cut to the paper card | David | "I'm David Shi, this is Srihari Kumaresan, and we built Red Pen, an app that checks every transaction a small city makes and marks only the ones a person should review." |
| 0:27 – 0:36 | App intro: the ledger streams by, then the review queue | Srihari | "When a clerk opens it, Red Pen has already read the whole ledger. Here it checked 1,885 transactions and found 30 worth a look." |
| 0:36 – 0:50 | Case file: duplicate payment, chart, reasons, mark reviewed | David | "Every finding explains itself in plain English. This janitorial invoice was paid twice, twelve days apart. The clerk adds a note and marks it reviewed, and the decision goes into a public audit log. Red Pen never blocks or changes a payment. People decide." |
| 0:50 – 0:56 | Vendors: merged spellings | Srihari | "It matches vendor names letter by letter, so a duplicate can't hide behind 'Bobs Plumbing' versus 'Bob's Plumbing Service.'" |
| 0:56 – 1:04 | Spending patterns: July snow bill circled | David | "A gradient-boosted model learns what each category should cost every month. Snow removal in July stands out right away." |
| 1:04 – 1:12 | Budget runway | Srihari | "A seasonal time-series forecast warns months ahead. Fire vehicle repairs will run out of budget in February, not after the books close in June." |
| 1:12 – 1:24 | Contracts: red underlines and margin notes, then the A-grade contract | David | "Before the council signs a contract, Red Pen marks risky terms, like automatic renewal with no new bid or a 6% yearly increase with no cap. Compare that with a well-written, competitively bid contract." |
| 1:24 – 1:31 | Sensitivity slider | Srihari | "Every threshold comes from Iowa State Auditor guidance, and each city can adjust it." |
| 1:31 – 1:40 | How it works: 10 of 10 scorecard | David | "To test it, we planted ten real-world problems in the sample town's books. Red Pen caught all ten, without being told where they were." |
| 1:40 – 1:50 | Back to the review queue | Srihari | "It runs in the browser, works on any city's spending export, and the data never leaves the computer." |
| 1:50 – 2:00 | End card | Both | "Red Pen. Every line, read." |

## Numbers in this script come from the app. Before recording, re-check them:
- transactions read and findings count: Review queue headline
- "caught all ten": How it works scorecard
- which category runs out first and when: Budget runway

If the engine changes, these numbers change. Update the script to match what's on screen.

## AI disclosure (required by the rules)
Say it in your written answers, not necessarily in the video: we used Anthropic's
Claude and OpenAI's Codex as coding assistants. Be specific about which parts you
wrote, changed, and tested yourselves, and make sure you can explain every algorithm.
